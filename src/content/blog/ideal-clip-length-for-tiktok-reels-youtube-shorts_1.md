<p class="answer">There's no single "perfect" length. TikTok now rewards clips over 60 seconds with more reach, Instagram Reels stops recommending anything past 3 minutes to new audiences, and YouTube Shorts has no ideal duration at all beyond fitting the idea without filler. The right length depends on the platform and what the clip is trying to do.</p>

Most advice on this still repeats a 2021 rule of thumb: "shorter is always better." That stopped being true a while ago. Here's what actually holds up per platform.

## TikTok: longer clips are getting more reach, not less

TikTok's own data (via Buffer's analysis of 1.1 million TikTok videos) found that videos over 60 seconds got **43.2% more reach and 63.8% more watch time** than videos between 30–60 seconds — and that users spend half their total viewing time on content longer than a minute. That doesn't mean pad every clip to a minute; it means don't cut a genuinely useful 90-second explanation down to 20 seconds just because "TikTok is a short-form platform." A rough split that works in practice:

- **15–45 seconds** — a single quick idea, a reaction, a hook-and-punchline
- **45–90 seconds** — a tutorial step or a mini-story with a beginning and payoff
- **1–3 minutes** — a deeper explanation, only when every second earns its place

## Instagram Reels: 3 minutes is a hard ceiling, not a target

Reels technically accepts uploads up to 20 minutes, but Instagram has said directly that **Reels over 3 minutes will not be recommended to new audiences** — past that point you're only reaching people who already follow you. Inside that ceiling:

- **15–45 seconds** for discovery-driven content aimed at people who've never seen the account before
- **30–90 seconds** for anything educational or how-to
- Stay under 3 minutes for a full explanation if you want any chance of non-follower reach

## YouTube Shorts: fit the idea, don't fill the runtime

Shorts caps out at 3 minutes as of the current spec, but YouTube's own guidance is less about a target number and more about a principle: use the length the content actually needs, cut the filler, and let audience retention data tell you what's working. In practice that tends to land around:

- **20–60 seconds** for a single focused idea
- **60–180 seconds** for a complete story, tutorial or explanation

## So how do you pick, before you've even cut the clip?

Work backwards from the moment, not a target duration. If you [picked your moments from a transcript first](/blog/turn-long-videos-into-shorts/), you already know roughly how long each one runs uncut — a strong 25-second answer to a real question doesn't need stretching to hit some arbitrary "ideal" number, and a 90-second story shouldn't be hacked down to 20 seconds just to fit a rule you read once. The three platform ranges above are guardrails for reach, not a formula to force every clip into.

One thing that does matter regardless of platform: don't let the length decision undo the framing decision. A clip trimmed to hit a target duration often gets cut mid-sentence or mid-gesture, which is a different (and more common) problem than the [aspect-ratio cropping issue](/blog/how-to-resize-video-for-tiktok-instagram-youtube/) covered elsewhere. Trim on a natural pause, not a stopwatch.

[Soft Clipper](/) scores moments for where they naturally start and end rather than chopping at a fixed timestamp, so a clip that wants to be 38 seconds stays 38 seconds instead of getting stretched or cut to hit a round number — you can still nudge in or out afterward if a platform's range calls for it.
